THE SCIENCE

What Gut Health Means

Most digestive complaints get one of two responses: a medication that reduces the symptom, or a workup that finds nothing structural and concludes the problem is stress. Both leave the patient managing something indefinitely.

Your digestive tract does considerably more than digest. It houses the majority of your immune tissue, manufactures neurotransmitters including a large share of your body’s serotonin, absorbs every nutrient you depend on, and maintains a barrier separating your bloodstream from everything you swallow.

There is usually a mechanism, and it is usually findable.

When that system falters, the effects reach well past digestion. Fatigue, brain fog, mood changes, skin problems, joint pain, hormone symptoms, and autoimmune activity all trace back to the gut in a meaningful number of patients.
